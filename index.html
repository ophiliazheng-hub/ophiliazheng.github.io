Gender pay & rank analysis · Course project

From a complex question to an evidence-based interpretation.

R · data.table · ggplot2 · 261 physician records

Frame the decision

Two questions

Measure the adjusted salary gap and examine whether women are less likely to hold full-professor rank.

Salary outcomeRank outcome

Audit the data

261 records

Checked missing values, blanks, duplicate physician IDs and impossible salary or experience values.

10 source variablesIntegrity checks

Prepare variables

Recode + derive

Labelled categorical fields, set reference groups, logged salary and derived growth and experience bands.

5 factor recodes4 derived fields

Explore patterns

Three views

Compared salary by gender and department, examined experience relationships and checked correlations.

Box plotFaceted trendCorrelation

Model salary

Five OLS models

Added experience, department, credentials and rank sequentially to test how the estimate changed.

Model academic rank

0.374× odds

Used logistic regression to estimate female-to-male odds of holding full-professor rank.

p .010895% CI .175–.797

Interpret responsibly

Pathway insight

Connected salary and rank evidence while separating statistical association from causal or legal proof.

LimitationsNext evidenceStakeholder story

The strongest signal is a possible career pathway behind the pay gap. Current rank is a proxy, not a recorded promotion decision, and the models do not prove discriminatory intent.
